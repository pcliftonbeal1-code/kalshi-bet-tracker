Business model under test: "We turn everyday people into experts. They own accounts that businesses pay them for every month." Software (AI + checklists) encodes rules-based expert work; unlicensed remote operators own recurring client accounts; founder sells.

## Q1. What do businesses currently pay (firms, consultants, software)? Is it recurring?

### Takeaway
Credentialing ($100–$500/provider/month), trucking compliance ($150–$400/month per carrier), grant work ($2k–$6k/month retainers) and certified payroll (software alone at $145/month to $8k/year, plus ~$7.50–$12/report) clear the $300/month bar with clear recurrence. Registered agent / annual report ($49–$300/year), SAM.gov registration (free from the government) and most Philadelphia city licenses are far below it. Sales tax filing is mid-priced, but per-return automated fees are already low ($35–$70/state).

## Q2. Is any license legally required for the work itself?

### Takeaway
None of the paperwork niches strictly requires a professional license for the operator's data-prep and filing work. The real limits are (a) unauthorized practice of law when advising on certification eligibility or contract insurance terms, (b) insurance-advice E&O exposure in COI work, (c) a federal PTIN if preparing federal tax returns for pay, (d) HIPAA BAAs for anything touching PHI, and (e) BEPP tune-ups, which require a city-certified tune-up specialist.

### Cited Findings
- PA has no state registration, exam or CE for paid tax preparers (third-party sources). The federal PTIN applies to anyone preparing federal returns for compensation, and PA requires the preparer PTIN on PA-40 returns from TY2020. — [LicenseMap PA](https://getlicensemap.com/states/tax-preparer/pennsylvania); [IRS PTIN FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/node/6322); [Thomson Reuters PA help](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/ultratax-cs/1040/states/pennsylvania/help-articles/pa.html)
- Credentialing firms often act as HIPAA business associates. A BAA is required before any PHI flows, and subcontractors handling PHI need their own BAAs (vendor/secondary sources). No source found saying credentialing specialists need a professional license. — [AccountableHQ](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/hipaa-compliance-for-medical-credentialing-services-requirements-and-best-practices); [HHS business associates](https://hhs.gov/ocr/privacy/hipaa/understanding/coveredentities/businessassociates.html)
- COI work: industry commentary says contract insurance-clause review ideally belongs to an attorney, then a broker. Disclaimers alone don't protect agencies from E&O, and certificate issuance "is not for untrained, unlicensed" staff (this concerns issuing certificates, which agents do, not reviewing them). No source found stating that reviewing or tracking COIs for a GC requires a producer license. — [Insurance Journal 2016](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines-national-section/2016/04/04/403444.htm); [IRMI](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/avoiding-common-insurance-certificate-errors); [Insurance Journal agencies](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2016/05/06/407744.htm)
- Lead-safe certifications must come from a PA-licensed inspector/risk assessor or an EPA-certified dust sampling technician. The operator could only manage the paperwork and scheduling, not the certification. — [phila.gov lead cert FAQ / search summary](https://www.phila.gov/2019-10-22-rental-property-lead-certification-law/)
- BEPP tune-up reports must come from certified tune-up specialists. — [phila.gov BEPP deadlines](https://www.phila.gov/programs/building-energy-performance-program/deadlines/)
- PA DOS says a Commercial Registered Office Provider is a registered status distinct from a "registered agent". Acting as a CROP requires registering with DOS. — [PA DOS CROPs](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dos/programs/business/information-services/commercial-registered-office-providers)
- Grant writing: no license, but commission-based compensation is barred by professional codes and can cause awards to be revoked (secondary sources). — [Grantable](https://grantable.co/how-much-does-a-grant-writer-cost); [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/grant-writing-fees)

### Inferences
- Certified payroll: no license is needed to compute and prepare WH-347 or PA certified payroll forms. The contractor's officer signs the Statement of Compliance, which keeps legal attestation with the client. This is a clean fit. Inference from the form structure, which has the contractor certify.
- Certifications (M/W/DSBE/DBE): filling out and assembling an application is fine. Advising an owner on whether they legally qualify (e.g., the new "individualized social and economic disadvantage" narrative under the 2025 DBE IFR) edges toward legal advice and carries fraud exposure if misstated. Operators must stay at "document prep and completeness".
- COI: tracking and requesting certificates and checking limits against a client-specified requirement matrix is clerical and low-risk. Interpreting whether endorsements satisfy contract language is the risky part. Keep the client's broker or attorney as the decision-maker.
- Credentialing data is mostly about providers, not patients, so PHI exposure is lower than in billing. Expect practices to demand a BAA anyway, and PHI risk rises if the work expands into billing or eligibility. Inference, not verified with HHS guidance.
- Sales tax returns: preparing state sales tax returns is not "tax return preparation" requiring a PTIN, because PTIN applies to federal returns. Not confirmed by a primary source here.

### Gaps
- No primary-source PA statute/regulation confirming that sales-tax return preparation by an unlicensed third party is permitted (strongly believed but unverified).
- No PA Insurance Department guidance found on whether unlicensed COI compliance review constitutes "insurance consulting" (PA does license some insurance consultants). This should be checked with PA Insurance Department rules before launching a COI niche.
- No source found on whether nonprofit 990-N/990-EZ prep requires a PTIN (990 series are information returns; believed PTIN required for some, unverified).

## Q3. Who are the buyers in Philadelphia and how many are there?

### Takeaway
The best-documented Philadelphia buyer pools are landlords (about 55,000 landlords, 288,000 units, 2021 estimate), PA motor carriers (77k–167k registered, conflicting counts; about 90% of US carriers run under 6 trucks), PA home-improvement contractors (70k+ registered around 2019), City-certified M/W/DSBE firms (31% of the city's $1.2B in contracted spend went to them), and contractors on City/State public works (weekly LCPtracker filers). Counts for COI buyers, credentialing buyers and BEPP buildings were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Landlords: Pew/Reinvestment Fund estimated about 55,000 landlords owning about 136,000 rental properties with 288,000 units. Only 2% of landlords have more than 25 units, but they own more than half of the units. About 45% of rental properties (30% of units) were unlicensed in 2020. — [Pew: Who are Philadelphia's Landlords? (2021)](https://www.pewtrusts.org/research-and-analysis/articles/2021/02/24/who-are-philadelphias-landlords); [Pew Rental Code Enforcement (2021)](https://www.pewtrusts.org/research-and-analysis/reports/2021/11/rental-code-enforcement-in-philadelphia)
- City public works: Philadelphia DOL Office of Labor Standards reviews weekly certified payrolls via LCPtracker and runs LCPtracker contractor trainings. A Jan–Jun 2026 compliance report lists 81 prevailing-wage violations. The city updates rate schedules frequently (latest found effective 10/21/2026). — [phila.gov prevailing wage violation page](https://www.phila.gov/services/working-jobs/worker-protections/wage-protections/report-a-prevailing-wage-violation/); [phila.gov DOL compliance report Jan–Jun 2026](https://www.phila.gov/media/20260916171415/dol-labor-policy-compliance-report-jan-june-2026.pdf) (snippet only; fetch blocked); [phila.gov city prevailing wage rates](https://www.phila.gov/documents/city-prevailing-wage-rates/)
- M/W/DSBE: the City Controller's May 2025 review found M/W/DSBE firms got 31% of $1.2B in city contract spend (FY23). Participation was 36.02% in FY19 and 30.08% in FY20. The registry is at phila.mwdsbe.com, but no firm count was found. — [Controller May 2025 MMM](https://controller.phila.gov/philadelphia-reports/may-2025-municipal-money-matters/); [OEO FY20 report](https://www.phila.gov/media/20201208211252/OEO-FY-20-Annual-Report-12.8.2020.pdf); [OEO FY23 report](https://www.phila.gov/media/20240818145152/Fiscal-Year-2023-Office-of-Economic-Opportunity-Annual-Report.pdf)

- PA motor carriers: counts conflict (164,570 / 76,548 / 167,367 registered; 79,504 active) across aggregator sites. Dec 2025: an L&I sweep hit 80 unlicensed contractor projects, a sign of enforcement intensity. — [data.gov L&I Trade Licenses](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/licenses-and-inspections-trade-licenses/resource/146e2efd-fb94-4089-825f-0a12495f19c7); [phila.gov L&I sweep Dec 2025](https://www.phila.gov/2025-12-26-citys-department-of-licenses-and-inspections-weekend-sweep-of-80-unlicensed-contractor-projects-reinforces-commitment-to-public-safety-and-construction-integr/)
- Philadelphia liquor: Philadelphia County has met its R-license quota (1 per 3,000 people; 2019 fact sheet). This means a fixed, sizable base of existing licensees that must renew or validate. No count found. — [phila.gov Restaurant Liquor License Fact Sheet](https://www.phila.gov/media/20190517120444/Rest-Liquor-License-Fact-Sheet.pdf)

- PA annual reports apply to essentially every PA corporation/LLC and foreign registrant. Administrative dissolution for non-filers begins in 2027 (6 months after due date per one law firm), and DOS mails notices ≥2 months before deadlines. — [PA DOS newsroom](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dos/newsroom/department-of-state-alerts-business-owners-about-new-annual-repo); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2025/4/your-pennsylvania-entitys-report-may-be-due-sooner-than-you-think-in-2025-and-beyond)

### Inferences
- Reachability without connections is best for: (1) public-works contractors, because the City publishes bid awards and OEO participation plans, LCPtracker trainings gather them, and prime GCs push certified payroll requirements down to subs; (2) landlords, through public rental-license and L&I data and landlord associations (e.g., HAPCO); (3) carriers, through public FMCSA data by ZIP; (4) the M/W/DSBE registry, which is public at phila.mwdsbe.com.
- Hardest to reach cold: healthcare practices (gatekept by office managers and saturated by RCM vendors) and liquor licensees (relationships with liquor attorneys).
- The 2027 PA annual-report dissolution enforcement is a dated, event-driven Philly/PA demand spike (2026–2027), but each job is tiny and likely a lead-gen wedge only.
- Small-landlord compliance (lead-cert expirations every 4 years, annual rental license renewals, Partners for Good Housing requirements) affects tens of thousands of small owners. Most own 1–5 units, so willingness to pay $300+/month is doubtful. Larger landlords (2% of owners, half of units) use property managers.

## Q5. Breaking points: what kills each niche?

### Takeaway
The biggest killers are policy shocks (DBE presumptions removed in Oct 2025; federal grant terminations), funded AI incumbents bundling the task into software the client already uses (Miter payroll, Jones COI, Assured credentialing, Avalara), free government help or scam stigma (SAM.gov, certifications), and low willingness to pay among micro-businesses (landlords, annual reports).

### Cited Findings
- DBE: USDOT's Oct 3, 2025 interim final rule removed race- and sex-based presumptions. All existing DBEs must be re-evaluated and recertified by showing individual social and economic disadvantage. Recipients use race-neutral measures until reevaluation finishes. A later final rule (FR doc 2026-19688) reportedly finalizes it with few changes. — [Federal Register IFR](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/03/2025-19460/disadvantaged-business-enterprise-program-and-disadvantaged-business-enterprise-in-airport); [Miller Nash](https://www.millernash.com/industry-news/usdot-issues-new-dbe-renewal-rules-no-more-automatic-race-or-gender-presumptions-to-qualify); [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/dot-issues-immediate-rule-overhauling-dbe-and-acdbe-programs-in-response-to-court-challenges/); [Final rule PDF](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-19688.pdf); [Cozen on FAR Part 19 + DBE](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2025/major-changes-far-part-19-and-dot-disadvantaged-business-enterprise-dbe-)
- City M/W/DSBE share fell from 36% (FY19) to about 30–31% (FY20, FY23). The program depends on local political commitment. — [OEO FY20](https://www.phila.gov/media/20201208211252/OEO-FY-20-Annual-Report-12.8.2020.pdf); [Controller 2025](https://controller.phila.gov/philadelphia-reports/may-2025-municipal-money-matters/)
- Grants: 21% of nonprofits lost a government grant or contract and 27% faced delays or freezes in early 2025 (Urban Institute, national). 66% of Philly nonprofits have ≤3 months of cash. — [Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/a-third-of-u-s-nonprofits-that-serve-communities-lost-government-funding-in-early-2025/); [NFF](https://nff.org/insights/survey-philly-nonprofits-working-to-meet-rising-demand-for-essential-services-amid-funding-pullbacks/)
- SAM.gov: imposter firms charging $399 for free renewals make any paid offering look like a scam. — [Truth in Advertising](https://truthinadvertising.org/articles/federal-filing-government-imposter-scam)
- Certified payroll: DOL is making the WH-347 easier (fillable form, annotated guide), and payroll platforms (Miter, Points North, Dapt) generate certified payroll directly from payroll data. — [DOL](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/whd/whd20251216-0); [Miter](https://www.constructiondive.com/press-release/20260930-miter-the-ai-platform-for-construction-raises-40-million-for-the-built-w/)
- Certified payroll also depends on public-construction volume. A court decision in 2026 reportedly affected the 2023 DBRA rule's material-supplier coverage (details unverified). — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/a-win-for-clarity-what-a-recent-federal-court-decision-means-for-davis-bacon-compliance/1816478)
- COI: E&O exposure if a missed endorsement leads to an uninsured loss. Industry says disclaimers alone don't protect. — [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2016/05/06/407744.htm)
- Credentialing: HIPAA BAA obligations and covered-entity liability for business-associate breaches. — [AccountableHQ](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/hipaa-compliance-for-medical-credentialing-services-requirements-and-best-practices)
- BEPP: tune-ups must be performed by certified specialists (engineering/mechanical firms). The cycle is every 5 years, so demand is lumpy by size tier (2026–2029). — [BEPP regs](https://www.phila.gov/media/20241004100137/OOS-BEPP-Regulations-Amended-20240710-Signed.pdf)
- HICPA: the PA AG suffered a cyber incident with a renewal grace period after Aug 2025, which shows the state process can change or pause. — [PA AG 2026 doc](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Inquiries-Arising-Since-Cyber-Incident.pdf)

### Inferences (per-niche breaking point, one line each)
1. Certified payroll: the client switches to an all-in-one construction payroll (Miter, Foundation, Points North) that generates CPRs. Also killed by public-works volume falling, or by the operator making a wage-rate classification error that triggers restitution. Mitigation: target subs and smaller primes on City/PennDOT/SEPTA/School District jobs that use QuickBooks or ADP and won't switch.
2. COI tracking: Jones/myCOI price down to SMB, or GCs' brokers do it free as a service. E&O risk from interpreting endorsements.
3. M/W/DSBE/DBE certification: federal/state/city retreat from race/gender programs (the DBE IFR already happened). Near term there's a recertification wave (demand spike), but long term the program shrinks and the narrative requirement edges toward legal advice.
4. SAM/bid prep: free APEX/PTAC help plus scam stigma. Bid prep (proposals) is better money but is relationship- and outcome-driven, not rules-based.
5. Landlord compliance: micro-landlords won't pay monthly. Lead cert itself requires licensed inspectors, so the operator role is calendar and uploads only.
6. Philly CAL/BIRT: the CAL is a one-time free license. BIRT/NPT filing is tax prep, which accountants own and which needs a PTIN for federal-linked work.
7. Food/PLCB: liquor attorneys and expediters own the relationships, and work is annual. Weak recurrence.
8. Contractor license renewals: annual/biennial, cheap, and handled by GCs themselves. Only works bundled with COI and certified payroll.
9. Sales tax: automated incumbents and per-return prices around $50. AI kills human value.
10. Trucking: a crowded, price-cut market ($45/month monitoring) with many fly-by-night "permit services". Small carriers churn and fail at high rates (not verified here). Drug-testing consortium work involves regulated parties (MROs, C/TPAs).
11. Grants: funding cuts, AI drafting, and ethics barring contingency pay. Post-award compliance reporting is the more defensible slice.
12. Credentialing: VC-funded AI moving down-market, plus HIPAA/BAA requirements. Still the highest-ARPU niche and the most proven "remote non-licensed operator" job category (credentialing specialists are commonly non-licensed remote staff; background knowledge).
13. Nonprofit filings: 990-N is free and trivial, and full 990s belong to CPAs. Low price.
14. PA annual report / CROP: commoditized at $95–$199/year nationally. A one-time 2027 dissolution scare spike only.
15. BEPP: engineering firms own tune-ups (certified specialists). Benchmarking data entry is small and infrequent.

### Gaps
- No data on churn rates for small carriers, small GCs, or credentialing clients.
- No verification of the DOL Sep 30, 2026 WH-347 old-form cutoff.
- The court decision affecting 2023 DBRA rule provisions was not verified.
